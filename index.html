A tale of one tax dollar

Once upon a time…

You wake up, clock in, and trade your hours for a paycheck. That makes you the hero of this story.

But at the top of a very tall beanstalk lives a giant who takes his share first. His name is Uncle Sam. He's big, he's loud, and he only ever says four words.

Fee. Fi. Fo. Fund.

This is the story of where his share goes, and how it finds its way back down to you.

    Chapter three of four

    Fo

    fo /fō/ short for formula

    The rules Uncle Sam uses to split the pot. Some money follows formulas based on population and need. Some follows people, like Social Security. Some follows federal jobs and contracts.

    Uncle Sam's ledger2023

    Only three states paid in more than they got back.

    New Jersey, Massachusetts, and Washington State. Every other state got back more than its people and businesses paid in. New Jersey's gap was the biggest: $18.9 billion, about $2,011 a person.

    Chapter four of four

    Fund

    fund /fənd/ verb

    What your dollar builds when it finally comes back down to you.

    Greetings from home$

    Home again.

    Add in your state and city taxes, and your dollars become the road you drive, the school down the street, the park on the corner, and the fire truck that shows up in minutes. Some come back as checks, like your grandparents' Social Security.

    Watch the map: the color spreads out from home.

    The happy ending

    …and they all lived happily, and fundedly, ever after.

    Turns out Uncle Sam isn't the villain. He's more like a very large accountant, and you get to check his math.

    You don't have to climb the beanstalk to have a say. Budgets are public, hearings are open, and you get a vote. One move for each chapter:

    1. Fee:Look at Box 2 of your W-2 to see how many of your dollars made the trip.
    2. Fi:Find your representative, who votes on the federal budget.
    3. Fo:See how your state's balance with Washington stacks up.
    4. Fund:Show up to a city budget hearing, and register to vote.
